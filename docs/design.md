# Design

## Users
Somebody at home, on a phone or a computer, adjusting a receipt printer: what it prints,
when, how dark, and printing something now. Short visits.

## Brand Personality
Name: **First Up**. In railway language "up" is towards London; it is also the first thing
seen when up. Voice: dry, plain, brief. Three words: handsome, early, dependable. Distinctive
with gravitas: never cartoonish, no jokes on controls.

## Aesthetic Direction
1930s British railway poster: flat colour, strong diagonals, London at sunrise. Each page is a
poster in a series and each drawing says something true (the train is made up of the chosen
blocks, the station clock shows the next print, signals show what is in use, taps are bell
beats, heat is a pressure gauge). The sky follows the sun at the weather place; the engine
takes the highlighted Tube line's colour. Type: Hammersmith One and Cabin, both descended from
Johnston and Gill, served from the Pi. Anti-references: grey-blue forms, cards in rows,
dropdown after dropdown, mascots.

## Design Principles
1. The picture informs; nothing is drawn only for decoration.
2. Plain label on every control. Wit stays in the drawing and the name.
3. Pick from what you can see (plates, beats, swatches) rather than from a closed list.
4. Reacts at once without scripts (`:has()`); respects reduced motion; works at 320 px.
5. Nothing from outside: no scripts, own fonts, strict content policy.
